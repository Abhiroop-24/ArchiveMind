ABSTRACT

Historical institutions and national archives preserve millions of fragile handwritten manuscripts, scientific proofs, and wartime ciphers. While photographic preservation mitigates organic decay, scholars face an acute discovery challenge: classical relational databases (RDBMS) excel at structured metadata querying and exact string matching but fail when confronted with degraded handwriting, archaic grammar, or conceptual semantics; standalone vector databases excel at high-dimensional similarity matching but lack relational schema constraints, referential integrity, multi-table joins, ACID transactions, and verifiable audit trails.

ArchiveMind addresses this fundamental dilemma by engineering a hybrid SQL-Vector architecture. Structured archival entities (Archives, Collections, Manuscripts, Authors, Views, Citations, Permissions) are normalized in an ACID-compliant schema. Handwriting stroke styles are transformed into dense 128-dimensional vectors, while OCR transcripts are indexed via Full-Text Search (FTS5 / tsvector). An adaptive rank-fusion scoring function ($Score = \alpha \cdot FTS + (1-\alpha) \cdot Cosine$) unifies keyword precision with visual penmanship forensics. The system incorporates database-level triggers enforcing chain-of-custody audit logging and stored procedures generating archival citations across Chicago, MLA, and APA standards.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background & Context

For centuries, human intellectual heritage has been preserved across handwritten mediums—from Sir Isaac Newton's draft calculations of celestial mechanics, to Leonardo da Vinci's mirror-script codices, Srinivasa Ramanujan's lost notebooks, and Alan Turing's Hut 8 naval decryption dispatches. While modern high-resolution scanning creates faithful digital replicas, researchers still cannot efficiently query documents by handwriting similarity, trace relational provenance, or recover documents when optical character recognition (OCR) transcriptions are noisy.

1.2 Motivation

Modern database engineering presents two diametric extremes: classical relational systems (PostgreSQL, MySQL, Oracle) and modern vector similarity engines (Qdrant, Pinecone, FAISS). Relational engines maintain strict ACID guarantees, granular role-based permissions, and foreign key cascades, but lack semantic nuance. Vector databases index neural embeddings but forfeit relational integrity. ArchiveMind bridges this chasm by presenting a unified hybrid architecture.

CHAPTER 2: PROBLEM DEFINITION

2.1 Limitations of Current Archival Repositories

  • OCR Fragility: Historical inks fade and bleed. A query for "universal gravitation" fails if OCR transcribed it as "gravitati0n" or "grauitatio".
  • Forensic Style Disconnect: Scholars studying unattributed manuscripts cannot query the database: "Find all folios whose penstroke contours match Da Vinci's left-handed mirror script with >90% cosine similarity".
  • Auditability Deficit: Vector-only search systems do not enforce legal chain-of-custody logging when rare national archives are inspected.

CHAPTER 3: ER MODEL

3.1 Entity Definitions & Attributes

The conceptual design of ArchiveMind comprises eleven relational entities designed in Third Normal Form (3NF):

  1. ROLE: (role_id, role_name)
  2. APP_USER: (user_id, username, password_hash, full_name, email, created_at, role_id)
  3. ARCHIVE: (archive_id, name, location, institution)
  4. COLLECTION: (collection_id, name, era, description, archive_id)
  5. ACCESS_PERMISSION: (permission_id, permission_type, granted_at, user_id, collection_id)
  6. DOCUMENT: (document_id, title, doc_type, date_written, ocr_text_cache, checksum_value, checksum_algorithm, physical_condition, language, image_path, ocr_status, digitized_at, collection_id, digitized_by)
  7. PERSON: (person_id, full_name, birth_year, death_year, biography)
  8. DOCUMENT_AUTHOR: (document_id, person_id, attribution_type, confidence_score)
  9. HANDWRITING_SAMPLE: (sample_id, vector_id, embedding, extraction_method, extracted_at, document_id)
  10. DOCUMENT_VIEW: (view_id, viewed_at, ip_address, document_id, user_id)
  11. CITATION: (citation_id, citation_text, citation_style, generated_at, document_id)

CHAPTER 4: ER TO RELATIONAL MAPPING

4.1 Steps of Algorithm for Chosen Problem

The standard 7-step mapping algorithm is applied as follows:

  • Step 1: Mapping of Regular Entities: Strong entities (ROLE, ARCHIVE, PERSON) are directly mapped into individual relations with surrogate primary keys.
  • Step 2: Mapping of Weak Entities: All entities contain unique identifiers; no partial-key weak entities exist.
  • Step 3: Mapping of 1:1 Relationships: No 1:1 relationships exist in this domain.
  • Step 4: Mapping of 1:N Relationships: The primary key of the parent entity is embedded as a foreign key on the "N" relation:
    • COLLECTION receives archive_id referencing ARCHIVE.
    • DOCUMENT receives collection_id referencing COLLECTION and digitized_by referencing APP_USER.
    • HANDWRITING_SAMPLE, DOCUMENT_VIEW, and CITATION receive document_id referencing DOCUMENT.
  • Step 5: Mapping of M:N Relationships: Associative relation DOCUMENT_AUTHOR is constructed with composite primary key (document_id, person_id) and descriptive attributes (attribution_type, confidence_score). Similarly, ACCESS_PERMISSION maps users to collections.
  • Step 6: Mapping of Multivalued Attributes: Multi-valued authors are factored into DOCUMENT_AUTHOR.
  • Step 7: Mapping of N-ary Relationships: All associations are strictly binary.

7.1 Simple Query with GROUP BY & AGGREGATE

SELECT c.era, d.physical_condition, COUNT(d.document_id) AS total_manuscripts,
       ROUND(AVG(LENGTH(d.ocr_text_cache)), 2) AS avg_ocr_length
FROM document d
JOIN collection c ON d.collection_id = c.collection_id
GROUP BY c.era, d.physical_condition
ORDER BY c.era ASC;

7.4 Correlated Subquery

Finds documents whose character length exceeds the average of documents within their OWN collection:

SELECT d1.document_id, d1.title, c.name, LENGTH(d1.ocr_text_cache) AS doc_len
FROM document d1
JOIN collection c ON d1.collection_id = c.collection_id
WHERE LENGTH(d1.ocr_text_cache) > (
    SELECT AVG(LENGTH(d2.ocr_text_cache))
    FROM document d2
    WHERE d2.collection_id = d1.collection_id
);

7.5 Nested Subquery

Finds historical authors whose works reside in institutions curating $\ge 2$ collections:

SELECT p.full_name, COUNT(da.document_id) AS doc_count
FROM person p
JOIN document_author da ON p.person_id = da.person_id
WHERE da.document_id IN (
    SELECT d.document_id FROM document d
    WHERE d.collection_id IN (
        SELECT c.collection_id FROM collection c
        WHERE c.archive_id IN (
            SELECT a.archive_id FROM archive a
            JOIN collection col ON a.archive_id = col.archive_id
            GROUP BY a.archive_id HAVING COUNT(col.collection_id) >= 2
        )
    )
)
GROUP BY p.full_name;

CHAPTER 8: STORED PROCEDURES & TRIGGERS

8.1 Stored Procedure: Archival Citation Generator

CREATE OR REPLACE PROCEDURE sp_generate_archival_citation(
    IN p_document_id INT, IN p_style VARCHAR(30)
) LANGUAGE plpgsql AS $$
DECLARE v_title VARCHAR; v_author VARCHAR; v_citation TEXT;
BEGIN
    SELECT d.title, COALESCE(p.full_name, 'Unknown') INTO v_title, v_author
    FROM document d LEFT JOIN document_author da ON d.document_id = da.document_id
    LEFT JOIN person p ON da.person_id = p.person_id WHERE d.document_id = p_document_id LIMIT 1;
    v_citation := v_author || ', ' || v_title || ' (' || p_style || ' Archival Preservation).';
    INSERT INTO citation (document_id, citation_text, citation_style) VALUES (p_document_id, v_citation, p_style);
    COMMIT;
END; $$;

8.2 Trigger: Chain of Custody & Checksum Integrity

CREATE TRIGGER trg_validate_doc_checksum
BEFORE UPDATE OF checksum_value ON document
BEGIN
    SELECT CASE WHEN LENGTH(NEW.checksum_value) < 16 THEN
        RAISE(ABORT, 'Cryptographic checksum corrupted. Rejecting update.')
    END;
END;
